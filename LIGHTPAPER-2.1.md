## 1. Why ASKNIGHTS exists

There has never been more art to look at. New digital works are made, shared and "minted" (published on a blockchain) every day. Some are extraordinary; many are not. Most people meet them in a feed, between price charts, countdowns and claims that a collection "sold out in minutes".

Three things make it hard to find what matters:

- **Noise.** Volume and attention are easy to manufacture. Neither tells you whether a work is any good.
- **Speculation.** When the conversation is about price, the art becomes an afterthought, and prices themselves can be faked.
- **Weak provenance.** It is not always clear who really made a work, which file *is* the work, whether a physical object truly belongs to its digital token, or whether the work will still be viewable in ten years.

ASKNIGHTS was founded in 2020 to answer a simple question: *what is worth looking at, and why?* Our working motto is **Collect → Curate → Exhibit**. We collect with care, we curate with evidence and refined judgement, and we exhibit in ways that respect the work: on screen, in immersive rooms and alongside physical objects.

Curation, as we practise it, is more than opinion. It is refined curatorial judgement: cultural perspectives seen in a new way, joined to a cultivated taste for what tends to last. Time is the hardest test any work faces. Our task is to recognise, early and with evidence, the work most likely to pass it.

A good curator helps you slow down, look and understand. The technology is new. The responsibility is not.

## 2. Web3 and NFTs in plain words

You do not need to be technical to enjoy digital art, but a little context helps you collect safely.

### What an NFT is

An **NFT** (non-fungible token) is a public record on a blockchain. A **blockchain** is a shared digital ledger that many computers keep in sync and that is very hard to alter after the fact. The NFT links to a particular artwork and says, in effect: *this item was created by this account, at this time, and is now held by this account.*

"Non-fungible" just means *unique or individually identifiable*. One pound coin is interchangeable with any other, but a signed print numbered 3 of 50 is not.

### What an NFT is not

- **It is not automatically the copyright.** Buying an NFT usually gives you ownership of that token, not the right to reproduce the artwork commercially. The artist's licence, if there is one, sets out what you may do.
- **It is not always the file itself.** Many NFTs point to an image or video stored elsewhere. If that storage disappears, the token can end up pointing at nothing. Where and how the media is stored matters.
- **It is not proof that the person who minted it is the artist.** Anyone can mint anything. Copies and impersonations exist.
- **It is not an investment product.** Some NFTs rise in price; many fall or never sell. Collect because you value the work.

### Wallets

A **wallet** is an app or device that holds the keys controlling your NFTs and digital currency. Think of it as a keyring rather than a purse. Your wallet comes with a **recovery phrase** (also called a seed phrase): a list of words that can restore it on any device.

> **Golden rule:** anyone who has your recovery phrase owns everything in your wallet. Never type it into a website, never share it, never photograph it. ASKNIGHTS will never ask for it.

### Provenance on-chain

**Provenance** is the history of a work: who made it, who has owned it and where it has been. In traditional art this lives in certificates, invoices, exhibition catalogues and archives, which can be lost or forged. On a blockchain, part of that history is public and time-stamped. Anyone can see the account that created a token and every transfer since.

That is powerful, but partial. A blockchain records what happened to a *token*. On its own it cannot tell you whether the creating account belongs to the real artist, or whether a physical object is the one described. That is where careful checking comes in.

### Why artists can benefit

- **Direct reach.** Artists can publish and sell to collectors anywhere, without needing a gallery's permission first.
- **A lasting public record.** The date and account of creation are recorded, which helps establish authorship over time.
- **Possible resale royalties.** Some platforms pay artists a share when a work is resold. This depends on the platform and is not guaranteed by the technology itself.
- **New forms.** Generative, interactive and phygital works can be collected and shown in new ways.

### Risks and common scams

A practical checklist for newcomers:

1. **Phishing links.** Fake mint pages and lookalike websites are the most common trap. Type addresses yourself or use bookmarks. Be wary of links sent in private messages.
2. **Wallet "approvals".** Some malicious sites ask you to sign a permission that lets them move your items. Read what you are signing. If you do not understand it, do not sign.
3. **Impersonation.** Scammers copy the names and pictures of artists, projects and support staff. Real teams rarely message you first.
4. **Copy-mints.** Someone mints a copy of a known work under their own account. Check the creator account against the artist's own website or verified channels.
5. **Fake volume.** Sales between connected wallets can make a work look more popular or valuable than it is.
6. **Broken links.** If the media is stored on a single ordinary web server, it may vanish. Longer-lasting storage options exist; ask where the work lives.
7. **Irreversibility, volatility and tax.** Transactions generally cannot be undone, prices can swing sharply, and buying or selling may have tax consequences where you live.

If something feels rushed ("only ten minutes left!"), slow down. Good art will still be good tomorrow.

## 3. What ASKNIGHTS does

### Curation

At heart, ASKNIGHTS is a curator of top digital and NFT art. We follow artists, studios, museums and exhibitions closely. We select works for their craft, ideas and staying power, not for their price chart. Each selection is a refined curatorial judgement: a fresh cultural perspective, informed by long looking, and an exacting eye for the work that will still reward attention when today's feed is long forgotten. Our own collection holds about 10,000 NFT artworks across several blockchains, including **Ethereum, Tezos and Solana**.

Our focus is on **redchips with bluechip potential**. By a *redchip* we mean an emerging artist or work with a growing, checkable record that is not yet widely established; by a *bluechip*, one whose standing is long and widely recognised. We look for work that can make the journey from redchip to bluechip over time. These words describe cultural standing and track record, not a forecast of price.

### The ASKNIGHTS Vault

We keep a vault for collection and art preservation purposes, not as an investment product. Its purpose is care: keeping the works we collect, and the records that explain them, safe and viewable for the long term. An NFT usually points to a file rather than containing it, so a work can disappear if the website or server hosting its media goes away. The vault exists so that the works in our care do not depend on any single platform staying online. It is not a fund, it is not offered to anyone as an investment, and it promises no financial return. It cares for both the digital works and the physical objects of our phygitals, the physical-digital pairs we collect.

### Exhibitions and Metaverse exposure

We believe exhibitions are proof. A work earns its place when it holds up on a wall or in a room, not just in a feed. We design immersive Metaverse rooms, meaning shared 3D spaces you can walk through in a browser or headset. We keep them small and calm, so they serve the art rather than overwhelm it: one primary space done properly, a tight selection of works, and labels that respect your time.

### Phygitals

A **phygital** work joins a physical object to a digital record. Done well, it is one experience: both halves matter, and the link between them is real and checkable. Done badly, it is a sticker on a product. We care about the link: who looks after the object, its condition over time, and evidence that token and object truly correspond.

### Editorial

We write about the art and ideas shaping this field, from museum conservation of digital works to generative art, immersive installations and authorship in the age of AI. Our editorial work includes the ASKNIGHTS Magazine, published at asknights.org/articles. It aims to inform and explain, never to tell anyone what to buy, and it never asks artists to explain or defend their work.

### Community

ASKNIGHTS is a community of artists, collectors and art lovers who care about lasting work, clear provenance and exhibitions that hold up. We share curated picks, exhibition news and a diary of shows worth seeing, through our Discord community and our LinkedIn page (linkedin.com/company/asknights).

### Access and the ASKNIGHTS token

The ASKNIGHTS token is a utility token. It gives access to ASKNIGHTS-DAO and a place on the whitelist for future airdrops and giveaways. ASKNIGHTS NFTs carry the same access utility. This is a matter of access only. We promise no airdrop or giveaway.

### Fees

ASKNIGHTS charges a 15% marketplace fee. Artists never pay to be curated. As the marketplace grows, we intend to lower the fee in stages, as far as the business can sustain without outside funding. This is an intention, not a commitment to any figure or date.

## 4. How we curate

### People perceive; machines predict

This is our founding principle, set out by Sebastian Clej. A person standing in front of a work *experiences* it: something happens that no description fully captures. A machine can only *predict* what a person might say about it. Both are useful, but they are not the same thing.

So the human experience comes first and is recorded in its own right. Machine predictions are treated as evidence to weigh, never as a vote. **AI informs; humans decide.**

### Three principles

1. **Human accountability.** No automated system decides what we select. A named, authorised ASKNIGHTS curator makes every decision, records the reasons and is accountable for it.
2. **Independence from any one supplier.** We are *AI-agnostic* and *blockchain-agnostic*. AI tools, blockchains and data services are interchangeable suppliers, chosen and re-chosen on how well they serve our work, not on reputation or loyalty. No single company's model defines our taste.
3. **Contradictions are surfaced, not averaged.** If three reviewers love a work and one raises a serious, evidenced concern, the average looks fine. It hides exactly what a curator most needs to see. We put the disagreement in front of the curator, along with what evidence would resolve it.

### How a work moves through the process

![How a work moves through ASKNIGHTS curation: five steps from evidence to a human decision, the red flags that cannot be outvoted, the four possible outcomes and our three principles.](curation-journey.png)

*Figure 1. The curation journey at a glance. AI informs; a named human curator decides.*

**1. Gather the evidence.** We assemble what is known about the work: its images or media, the artist's own statements, the public blockchain record and relevant sources. Each fact is marked as confirmed or not, and where sources disagree we say so. We do not fill gaps with guesses.

**2. Independent AI views.** Several different AI systems each review the work separately, without seeing one another's answers. They first look at it *without the artist's name*, and then again with it. This lets us see when fame, rather than the work, is moving an opinion.

**3. Specialist perspectives, including a devil's advocate.** Focused questions are handled separately, each looking at one aspect of the work, from craft and ideas to provenance and preservation. One perspective has a single job: to argue *against* selection and find what could make it a mistake.

**4. Synthesis that shows the disagreements.** A synthesis step brings these views together and highlights where they conflict. Some concerns are **red flags that cannot be outvoted**: doubts about authorship, provenance, copying, preservation or a phygital link. However enthusiastic everyone else is, a credible red flag stays on the table until it is resolved with evidence.

**5. A human decision.** The curator reviews everything, including the disagreements, and records a decision with reasons. A curator who always agrees with the tools is a warning sign, not a success.

### Checks, not guarantees

Our earlier documents, written by our founder Sebastian Clej, introduced three ideas: Proof-of-Authenticity, Proof-of-Originality and Proof-of-Inspiration. We now describe them as **checks**. A check can be carried out carefully, but it can never give absolute certainty.

- **Authenticity check.** Can we trace a clear chain from the artist, to the account that created the token, to the token, to the work itself, and, for phygitals, to the physical object? The chain is only as strong as its weakest link. A missing link is reported, not glossed over.
- **Originality check.** We search for earlier works that this one may substantially copy. A clean result means *"we found no match within the sources we could search"*. It does not mean *"guaranteed original"*. Copies from sources we cannot see may still exist.
- **Inspiration credit.** Influence is not a flaw. Where evidence is good, we aim to credit what a work draws on and to separate open homage from unacknowledged copying. Artists can confirm, add to or contest these credits.
- **Preservation check.** Where is the work stored, how could it be lost, and can its controller change it?

When a check comes back clean, we say it was *checked*, and we say what was checked and when. We do not issue certificates of authenticity, and our checks are not a warranty.

### Four possible outcomes

| Outcome | What it means in plain words |
|---|---|
| **Select** | Yes. We are glad to stand behind this work and show it. |
| **Conditional** | Yes, with conditions. For example, a disclosure, better storage of the media or a missing document supplied first. |
| **Defer** | Not yet. Something important is unresolved, and we would rather wait for evidence than guess. |
| **Decline** | Not for us, at least for now. It is a judgement about fit and evidence at this moment, not a verdict on an artist's worth. |

Curation is refined judgement, exercised openly and with evidence, and like any serious judgement it can be revisited. Our decisions are recorded and never quietly edited. If we change our mind, we record a new decision that refers to the old one.

## 5. What this means for you

### For collectors

- **A filter you can understand.** We explain why a work is in, not just that it is in.
- **Honesty about gaps.** If a fact could not be confirmed, we say so rather than leaving you to discover it later.
- **Attention to longevity.** We care whether a work will still be viewable, and whether a phygital's physical half is properly cared for.
- **No price talk dressed up as curation.** We never forecast prices or suggest that a work will rise in value.

### For artists

- **Judged on the work.** We deliberately test whether fame is swaying an assessment, which helps artists who are not yet famous.
- **Credit for your sources, and for you.** Our inspiration credits aim to recognise lineage properly, and you can respond to them.
- **Exhibitions that respect the art.** We design rooms and labels around the work, not around the platform.
- **Reasons, not silence.** Every outcome, including Conditional and Defer, is recorded with its reasons. You are never expected to explain or justify your work to us; the work speaks for itself.
- **Your rights stay yours.** Owning an NFT does not give us, or anyone, your copyright. We aim to respect the terms of your licence and to credit your work whenever we show it.

### For members and art lovers

- **A calmer way in.** Curated picks, exhibition diaries and editorial that explains rather than sells.
- **Places to look, and people to talk to.** Immersive rooms to visit wherever you are, and conversation about art, not price chatter.

### For museums, galleries and partners

A transparent, evidence-led method with human accountability, and respect for your decisions: in partner exhibitions, partner curators decide for their own venue.

## 6. Our commitments

1. **A human is always accountable.** Every curatorial decision is made and signed off by an authorised ASKNIGHTS curator. We intend to widen the circle of curators over time.
2. **Honesty about small numbers.** Our method is young. We will not claim it "works" on the strength of a handful of examples. When we share findings, we will say how many works and people they rest on, and what that does and does not allow us to conclude.
3. **Periodic reviews of our own decisions.** We intend to revisit decisions after roughly 6, 12 and 24 months: do they still stand, did problems emerge later, did our tools mislead us?
4. **Privacy by design.** The views of our curators and partners are personal data and are treated as such. We do not use biometrics such as facial or emotion tracking.
5. **Blockchain-agnostic and AI-agnostic, honestly.** We already hold and curate work on Ethereum, Tezos and Solana. Full support for every chain is a journey, not a finished fact, and we will say clearly what is supported.
6. **Market data is context, never quality.** Price, volume and "sold out" claims never count as evidence that a work is good.
7. **Synthetic is never confused with real.** Where we test our systems with simulated or AI-generated data, it is labelled as such and never treated as a real person's response.

## 7. What we have left behind

ASKNIGHTS has grown up alongside this field, and our thinking has changed. Earlier versions of our public material included ideas we no longer pursue. We have let them go because they do not serve good curation:

- **Price prediction.** We no longer aim to assign past, present and future prices to artworks. Curation is more credible when it does not forecast prices.
- **Investment products.** We are not building an art fund or any pooled investment. Our collection and preservation vault (section 3) exists to care for works over the long term, not to generate returns.
- **Play-to-earn.** Our Metaverse work is about exhibitions, not games with financial rewards.
- **Token promises.** We make no promises about any token. The ASKNIGHTS token is a utility token for access only, as set out in section 3. We promise no airdrop or giveaway.
- **"Fully autonomous" curation.** Earlier we imagined a system "run by algorithms". We now believe the opposite: AI informs, humans decide.

What remains is the core: thoughtful curation, honest provenance, phygitals done properly and exhibitions worth visiting.

## 9. Glossary for newcomers

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Blockchain** | A shared public ledger kept by many computers, which is very hard to alter after the fact. Ethereum, Tezos and Solana are examples. |
| **NFT** | A unique token on a blockchain that links to a specific work and records who created and holds it. |
| **Mint** | To create an NFT on a blockchain, publishing the work's record for the first time. |
| **Wallet** | An app or device that holds the keys to your NFTs and digital currency. |
| **Recovery (seed) phrase** | The secret words that restore your wallet. Whoever has them controls it. Never share them. |
| **Smart contract** | A small program on a blockchain that runs automatically, for example to create tokens or handle a sale. |
| **Provenance** | The history of a work: who made it, who owned it, where it has been. |
| **On-chain / off-chain** | Recorded on the blockchain itself, or stored elsewhere (such as a website or storage network). |
| **1/1 and editions** | A 1/1 is a single unique work. An edition is one of a set of identical copies. |
| **Royalties** | A share of a resale price paid to the artist, where a platform supports it. |
| **Phygital** | A work that joins a physical object to a digital record. |
| **Metaverse room** | A shared 3D space, viewed in a browser or headset, where works can be exhibited and visited. |
| **Curation** | Refined judgement about which works deserve attention and are likely to endure, made openly, with evidence, by accountable people. |
| **Redchip / bluechip** | Art-world shorthand. A redchip is an emerging artist or work with a growing record; a bluechip is long and widely established. We use the terms for cultural standing, not price. |
| **ASKNIGHTS token** | A utility token that gives access to ASKNIGHTS-DAO and a place on the whitelist for future airdrops and giveaways. ASKNIGHTS NFTs carry the same access utility. No airdrop or giveaway is promised. |
| **ASKNIGHTS-DAO** | The ASKNIGHTS community organisation, which the ASKNIGHTS token and ASKNIGHTS NFTs can be used to enter. |
| **Wash trading** | Trading a work between connected wallets to fake demand or price. |
